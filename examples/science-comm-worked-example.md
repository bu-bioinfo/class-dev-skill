# Worked Example — Science Communication Syllabus

Condensed excerpts from `context/Science Communication Syllabus.docx` ("Professional
Development in Science Communication," work-in-progress). This is the closest
real-world model to what this skill should help instructors produce: it combines
TILT, a differentiated AIAS policy, and an instructor self-disclosure in one
document. Use it as a reference for tone and structure, not as content to copy
verbatim into an unrelated course.

## Instructor's own AI-disclosure statement (verbatim)

> "This syllabus was developed with AI assistance at Levels 2 and 3 of the AI
> Assessment Scale (Perkins, Furze, Roe & MacVaugh, 2024). AI tools were used for
> brainstorming, outlining, and suggestions; all content was generated manually
> first before AI suggested edits and revisions approved by the instructor. AI will
> never be used to evaluate or provide feedback on any student assessment in this
> course. All feedback is conducted solely by the instructor."

Notice the structure: names the framework + version → names the actual activities
(brainstorming, outlining, suggestions) → states the workflow order (manual first,
AI-assisted revision second, instructor-approved) → draws an explicit, separate
boundary (never for evaluating/feedback).

## TILT structure (worked pattern, repeated per learning objective)

Each learning objective in the syllabus gets three labeled sub-sections:

- **Purpose — Why this matters**
- **Task — What you will do**
- **Criteria — How you'll know you're succeeding**

Objectives fleshed out this way in the source syllabus: public-speaking
confidence; tailoring content to an audience; visual design and delivery technique.
Each is concrete and anchored to a specific, nameable skill rather than a vague
course goal.

## Differentiated AIAS policy (worked pattern)

The syllabus states its rationale before the table:

> "Rather than a single blanket policy, each component of this course has an
> assigned level that reflects its learning goals... The goal is to use AI in ways
> that serve your learning rather than substitute for it."

Then assigns levels per component — notably:

- **Level 1 (No AI)** for all self-evaluations, reflections, and peer feedback,
  with the rationale stated inline: "only you can reflect on your own experiences."
- **Levels 2–4** for slide generation and talk preparation, where AI-assisted
  production is appropriate to the skill being built.
